THE PROCESSING PROBLEM

A resource is rarely
ready to use.

Valuable minerals come mixed with other material. Before extraction, we need to understand and prepare that feed.

Beneficiation changes the composition or properties of a feed by separating particles. It can mean enriching a mineral fraction, classifying material by size, or removing material that complicates downstream processing.

I focus on physical separation and dry beneficiation: using differences in particle behaviour while reducing reliance on water. Lunar processing takes those constraints further, raising questions about vacuum, gravity, dust and material handling.

01 / TRIBOCHARGING & ELECTROSTATIC SEPARATIONMODEL → DESIGN → EXPERIMENT

Contact.
Charge.
Separate.

The path a particle takes through a tribocharger changes the contacts it experiences. Those contacts influence the charge it acquires.

From a contact model to a physical separator

My PhD investigated tribocharging through modelling and experiments. In discrete element modelling, I examined particle contacts to inform tribocharger design, then evaluated an optimised design in a free-fall electrostatic separator.

What the experiments showed

Under terrestrial test conditions, pure silica and ilmenite acquired negative and positive charge respectively. The tribocharger changed their motion through the electric field. In JSC-1 simulant, increased density in some collectors indicated enrichment of denser constituents.

The useful result is the connection between charger design, particle charging and measurable separation behaviour, with performance evaluated for pure minerals, binary mixtures and a more complex simulant.

What this explainsContacts influence charge acquisition. The field acts on charged particles, so changing charging conditions can change where material reports to a collector.

What my research investigatesHow contact history and tribocharger design influence charging, and how simulations can be tested against separator experiments.

Where the simplification stopsCharging follows an illustrative saturation curve, q* = 1 − exp(−4c), where c is contact exposure from 0 to 1. The coefficient, charge magnitudes and motion are not fitted to experimental data. Real feeds have distributions of charge, size and contact histories.

02 / VIBRATION-INDUCED SIZE CLASSIFICATIONAPOLLO REGOLITH & SIMULANTS

Look inside
a shaken bed.

Size classification conditions a feed for the next process. Vibration offers a way to explore that without a process fluid.

In our 2025 study, we compared Apollo 15 sample 15601 with JSC-1, LMS-1 and TUBS-M simulants using sealed-vial vibration and micro-CT. All tests were conducted under Earth gravity.

What we found

The Apollo sample formed a three-layer structure. Simulants responded differently: separating readily did not necessarily make a simulant representative. Particle-size distribution helped explain the different behaviours.

These are small-sample, terrestrial experiments. Their value is in understanding material response and evaluating simulants; they do not establish lunar operating performance.

Small grains find gaps.
Large grains find the top.

Shaking can open spaces in a granular bed. Smaller grains can settle into gaps beneath larger grains, leaving the larger grains higher in the bed over successive cycles. 03 / TERRESTRIAL DRY BENEFICIATIONMATERIAL → SEPARATION → FLOWSHEET

From the ore
to the flowsheet.

A substantial strand of my work concerns terrestrial dry mineral processing: connecting material properties to separation performance and practical process choices.

I work across characterisation, experimental investigation, performance analysis and flowsheet development. The questions extend beyond whether a separator works: what feed does it suit, how sensitive is it to conditions, and how does it fit into the whole process?

Start with the material

Particle size, mineral associations, texture and physical properties inform which separation mechanisms are plausible. I connect characterisation evidence with the design and interpretation of processing tests.

Compare the mechanisms

My experience spans evaluating dry separation options, including magnetic, electrostatic and density-based methods, and understanding their role alongside size classification and feed preparation.

Ask how dry is dry

Moisture and environmental conditions matter to dry processing. I investigate how material conditioning and changing feed properties influence behaviour, so that a technology is assessed in the conditions it needs to operate.

Diagnose the separation

Evaluate the trade-offs

The aim is to make better use of mineral resources while reducing reliance on water and understanding the wider consequences of a processing choice.
