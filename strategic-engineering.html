Separation performance affects feed quality and downstream requirements. Strategic engineering asks how those technical choices fit into a viable resource system.

A process can work technically and still be the wrong investment. Demand, operating costs and deployment timing can change the answer.

My work with the Strategic Engineering Laboratory at Imperial connects resource engineering to decisions under uncertainty. In the ESA-supported space resources value-chain programme, I work on modelling the business case for lunar resource systems: connecting technical choices with costs, markets and deployment strategies.

Model, configuration assumptions & limits

Production follows the limiting stage

Feed processed = min(available feed, processing capacity, delivery capacity ÷ yield, demand ÷ yield). Saleable output = processed feed × yield. Yield is the saleable mass fraction of the feed, rather than a mineral recovery calculated from a feed grade.

The chain throttles extraction and processing to avoid making unsold product. There are no inventories, transport losses or separate actor margins. Increasing yield may reduce feed consumption when delivery or demand limits output.

Discounting is explicit

All investment occurs at year 0. Full annual operating cash flows occur at year-end from the first operating year, for the chosen operating life. NPV = −investment + Σ[(revenue − operating cost) ÷ (1 + r)year]. There is no revenue or operating cost before operation starts. Once operating, each chain runs at its feasible output for the full operating life, even if its annual cash flow is negative; shutdown and abandonment decisions are not modelled.

Prices and costs stay constant in real terms; the discount rate is real. Taxes, financing, working capital, replacement investment, ramp-up, residual value and closure costs are omitted. This is a deterministic, pre-tax, unlevered DCF illustration.

The yield adjustment adds the same percentage points to all three yields. Capital and operating multipliers scale their respective costs; the operating multiplier includes extraction and fixed costs.

Break-even price holds production and demand fixed and solves for NPV = 0. The highest NPV identifies the best of these three configurations under the selected assumptions. It is not an optimisation over all possible value chains, or a model from the ESA programme.
