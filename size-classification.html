Size matters.
So does motion.

Understand how grain sizes and vibration influence the structure of a granular bed.

Size classification prepares a feed for downstream processing. My vibration research investigates size segregation in Apollo regolith and simulants: a mechanism that can inform classification, rather than a demonstration of a complete classifier.

01 / VIBRATION-INDUCED SIZE CLASSIFICATIONAPOLLO REGOLITH & SIMULANTS

Look inside
a shaken bed.

Size classification conditions a feed for the next process. Vibration offers a way to explore that without a process fluid.

In our 2025 study, we compared Apollo 15 sample 15601 with JSC-1, LMS-1 and TUBS-M simulants using sealed-vial vibration and micro-CT. All tests were conducted under Earth gravity.

What we found

The Apollo sample formed a three-layer structure. Simulants responded differently: separating readily did not necessarily make a simulant representative. Particle-size distribution helped explain the different behaviours.

These are small-sample, terrestrial experiments. Their value is in understanding material response and evaluating simulants; they do not establish lunar operating performance.

What this model can tell us

This is an exploratory demonstration of granular segregation: spherical rigid bodies move under gravity and contact forces in a vibrating cylindrical vial. No upward trajectory is imposed. A visual cutaway reveals the intruder while all grains remain in the calculation.

The model omits air drag, cohesion and irregular grain shapes. The amount of rise depends on numerical settings. It is not calibrated to regolith or validated against the published experiments above.

The centre-of-mass plot measures vertical height relative to the moving container base. Cycle averages show the trend across each vibration cycle; instantaneous values and downloaded results retain the bouncing motion. The final point follows a short rest after vibration stops.
